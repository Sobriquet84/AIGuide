AI at Atlas: Your Quick Guide

The practical do’s and don’ts for using AI at work. AI tools like Claude and ChatGPT are already part of how we work — used well, they help us move faster and do better. Used carelessly, they can put confidential data, our clients, and our people at risk. This guide covers what you need day-to-day, alongside the full Atlas HXM AI Policy.

1What counts as “AI”

Any tool that uses machine learning or generative AI to create content, summaries, recommendations, or decisions — standalone tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot) and AI features built into everyday tools (BambooHR, Greenhouse, HubSpot, Zendesk, Teams, and similar).

2Things to remember

  • You’re accountable. AI may help you draft, but ultimately, you decide. Always review and verify before you send, publish, or act on anything AI produces.
  • Use Approved tools only on your Atlas account. Personal ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini/Copilot accounts are off-limits for any Atlas or client work — even small, harmless-feeling tasks.
  • For access to any tool, submit a ticket to IT or email help@atlashxm.com.
  • Keep it separate. Don’t use your Atlas AI account for personal tasks, and don’t use personal accounts for Atlas work.
  • Mind what you share. Sensitive data (employee records, health info, comp, client-confidential info) never goes into an AI tool — see the data table below.
  • Be honest about AI’s role. If AI meaningfully shaped a decision or piece of work, be ready to say so.
  • Building an app that you need to share, or that involves an expense? Email help@atlashxm.com for review and approval.

3What can I put into an AI tool?

When in doubt, share less, not more:

Public

Marketing content, public website copy, published info

Yes — no restrictions

Internal / Confidential

Internal memos, process docs, project plans, non-sensitive PII

Only in Approved tools with an enterprise account. Redact where you can.

Sensitive / Restricted

Employee records, salary, address, birthdates, health data, discipline, legal advice, trade secrets, client-confidential data

No — never put this into any AI tool

4Quick reference: green, yellow, red light

Green — go ahead

  • Drafting announcements, FAQs, or other non-decision communications
  • Summarizing public or internal documents you already have access to
  • Research, learning, and brainstorming (verify anything before you rely on it)
  • Proofreading, formatting, and improving tone on content you wrote
  • Drafting presentations, meeting notes, and status updates

Yellow — ask first / get sign-off

  • AI-drafted job descriptions or employee communications (HR review required)
  • Connecting AI to a system like SharePoint, HubSpot, or Zendesk (needs CTO/CISO approval)
  • Uploading financial reports, contracts, or call/meeting transcripts (enterprise account + human review)
  • Any new AI tool, agent, or integration must follow the TPRA process

Red — never

  • Entering employee, candidate, or client-confidential data into any non-approved AI tool
  • Using AI to analyze emotion, tone, or facial expressions of candidates or employees
  • Letting AI infer protected characteristics from data
  • Letting AI be the sole decision-maker on anything affecting someone’s employment, pay, or opportunities
  • Creating deepfakes or synthetic media of a real person without consent
  • Presenting AI-generated work as your own unaided work when attribution matters

5AI in meetings

  • Always tell everyone in the meeting and get consent before recording or transcribing.
  • Only use approved note-taking tools — not a personal note-taker bot.
  • See an AI bot you didn’t invite? Pause and ask before continuing.

6Not sure? Ask.

If you’re weighing whether something is okay to run through AI, pause and check the AI Policy, or reach out before you proceed. One email is a lot safer than a mistake — asking is always the right move.
